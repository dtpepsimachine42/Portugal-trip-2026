Aug 1–8

The Algarve

Seven nights on the cliffs and coves of the western Algarve — beaches, sea caves, and a courtyard pool to come home to. Two days left wide open on purpose.

Dates
Aug 1 – 8
Base
Ana Ana villa, Portimão
Group
7 adults · 4 kids (6, 4, 3, 1½)
Wheels
2 rental cars
How this week is built. Everything active happens in the morning; mid-afternoon is for the pool while the Algarve bakes. Beaches and boats sit on the coast (milder); inland stops like Silves get hot, so they're timed late.
  • Book ahead now: the Aug 4 catamaran, Zoomarine tickets, and any adults-only dinners fill up in peak season.
  • Marinha's staircase (~200 steps) is a lot with little kids in tow and in the heat — you'll see it best from the water and the clifftop viewpoint.
  • Two open days (Aug 4 & 6) are deliberate breathing room — and the natural window for Daniel's dive.
Day by day

The week

SAT1AUG
Arrival · keep it to nothing

Land, settle, exhale

Plan on the flight plus the drive from Lisbon airport eating the day ≈2.5–3 hr drive

Get to the villa, drop the bags, and let the kids loose in the courtyard pool. The only "plan" is a short walk to dinner — Dona Ana is steps from the door, or wander to the riverside spots under the old bridge for grilled fish and piri-piri. Early night.

SUN2AUG
Easy beach + a hilltop castle

Praia da Rocha, then Silves

Morning — Praia da Rocha, Portimão's own beach: big, flat, sandy, with a boardwalk and cafés. Gentle first-day water. ≈10 min / 15 min walk

Late afternoon — Silves, once the heat breaks (~4–5pm). Clamber the red-sandstone Moorish castle, see the old cathedral, and reward everyone with riverside gelato. ≈20 min

If the beach wipes everyone out, Silves keeps perfectly for one of the open days.

MON3AUG
The big kid day

Zoomarine

Full day — Marine park meets water park: dolphin and sea-lion shows, aquariums, gentle rides, and pools. It's the one attraction that lands with every age in the group. ≈35 min (Guia)

Buy tickets online, arrive at opening, and pack swimsuits — the pool areas are half the fun and your midday heat escape.

Dinner: Guia is Portugal's piri-piri chicken capital, five minutes away.

Zoomarine tickets
TUE4AUG
Open · pool day

A free day (and a dive)

Villa, pool, gelato, a slow wander into town. A few days in, everyone's over the jetlag — a good buffer before the busier back half of the week.

Daniel's dive: the morning to peel off for the reef dive while the crew has an easy pool day — back for the afternoon. If it's a St. Vincent-level day, Thursday's open to do it again. See Adults only → Scuba.

WED5AUG
On the water

Benagil & Marinha coastal cruise

Morning — A catamaran from Portimão marina (right by the villa): stable, shaded, with a swim stop in the calm water off Praia da Marinha. Book a morning departure for calmer seas and better light in the cave. marina ≈5 min

Afternoon — Pool. Optional flat clifftop viewpoint over Marinha on the way home (no stairs).

You're 11 people — a private charter may beat a shared boat: one boat, your own pace for naps and swims. Note the cave rules: you view Benagil from the water (no swimming or landing inside).

THU6AUG
Open · chill

Another day off the clock

Pool, a favorite-beach revisit, gelato, an unhurried lunch. Your second protected buffer — resist the urge to fill it.

Optional — Carvoeiro: if anyone's restless, the easiest low-key outing is the cliff-framed village of Carvoeiro — a small sandy town beach right in the center, cafés around the square, and the Algar Seco boardwalk over dramatic rock pools and arches just east of it. Pair it with Praia do Vale de Centeanes, a quieter cove beach a few minutes on with its own car park and calm, sheltered water. ≈15–20 min

Daniel's dive rebook slot: if Tuesday's reef dive was worth repeating, go again while the crew takes it easy. See Adults only → Scuba.

FRI7AUG
A day in Lagos · last day

Ponta da Piedade grottoes

Morning — A small traditional boat from Lagos Marina threads into the gold-cliff grottoes (~1 hr, family-friendly). ≈40 min

After — Lunch in Lagos's pretty old town, then flat, gentle Meia Praia for the afternoon. A fitting last hurrah — and a candidate for a kid-free lunch at Vila Joya or Ocean if a few of you want to split off.

This and Wednesday are both coastal-grotto trips, so if you'd rather vary it, swap in dolphin watching from Lagos instead.

SAT8AUG
Travel to Lisbon

North to Belém, with a beach lunch

Leave after breakfast. It's ~2.5 hr straight up the A2, so break it in the back half at Comporta — a flat, endless beach with good restaurants right on the sand. From there it's about an hour on, over the 25 de Abril bridge to the hotel. Comporta ≈2 hr from Portimão

Prefer minimal fuss? Alcácer do Sal is a quick riverside coffee stop on the motorway. Bigger detours (Arrábida/Setúbal, Évora) are worth it only if you want the stop to be the day.

Adults only

Options to drop into the open days (Aug 5 & 7) or any kid-free window. Book the dinners and the dive well ahead.

Scuba — reef & marine-life boat dive Daniel only

A morning boat dive on the Algarve's rocky reefs — think octopus, cuttlefish, moray and conger eels, sea bream, and the odd seahorse, with light playing through the sandstone. Sites sit at ~8–18m, squarely in Open Water range, so this is the right call while the family has their day.

Half-day, 2-tank · depart ~8:00, back ~13:00 · water 18–23°C (5mm wetsuit provided) · viz ~5–15m · gear rental available · bring your cert card + logbook

Two honest notes. The famous Ocean Revival wrecks (off Alvor) sit around 30m, and every operating center runs them as Advanced-only — so those wait until you've got an AOW cert. Today is the reefs, which are still excellent. And since it's been ~10 months, ask for a quick refresher / check dive on the first descent.

When: Aug 4 morning — a free pool day for the family, and you're over the jetlag. If it's amazing, Aug 6 is open to do it again.

  • Diver's Cove (Carvoeiro, ~15–20 min) — the Algarve's oldest PADI center, family-run, reef boat dives sized to your level. The natural first pick.
  • Indigo Divers or Easydivers (Albufeira marina) — small-group reef dives if Diver's Cove is booked.
Diver's Cove

Fine dining 2 nights, tops

  • Vila Joya — 2 Michelin stars, on the beach near Albufeira. Biggest table seats 6; open Mar–Nov. ≈35 min
  • Ocean at Vila Vita Parc — 2 stars, Porches. ≈20 min
  • Bon Bon — 1 star, Carvoeiro. ≈20 min
  • Vista — 1 star, Praia da Rocha — the closest, basically in Portimão. ≈10 min

Seven Hanging Valleys hike

The standout coastal walk — ~6 km of clifftop between Praia da Marinha and Praia de Vale Centeanes, passing directly over Benagil's skylight. Go at dawn before the heat and crowds; it's free.

≈20 min to the Marinha trailhead · 2–3 hr · sturdy shoes, water

Kayak into Benagil · Dolphin watching

Kayak or SUP is the intimate way into the cave the catamaran only glimpses — book a calm early slot from Benagil or Carvoeiro. For a different flavor, a dolphin-watching trip out of Portimão or Lagos is a reliable crowd-pleaser (and works with kids too, if you'd rather share it).

Off-road quad to Fóia Adults · book ahead

The adrenaline highlight. Algarve Riders' 3-hour Monchique quad tour is the standout: 300cc ATVs up the mountain to Fóia, the Algarve's highest point (902m), across varied terrain, steep climbs, and wide-open trails past old ruins — with breathtaking views at the top. All experience levels, small group (max 6).

Departs the Autódromo near Portimão · free hotel pickup within ~15 km · ~3 hr · B driver's licence + refundable deposit

Fun two-up if someone wants to ride along. Prefer a roll cage? They run double-seat buggies too. (Albufeira operators do easier countryside loops to Paderne Castle if you want gentler.)

Algarve Riders

Monchique spa · Golf

The calmer counterpoint: Caldas de Monchique is a cool, green thermal-spa village 40 minutes inland — a restorative half-day. And if anyone plays, the western Algarve has serious golf (Morgado and Alto near Portimão; Quinta do Lago and San Lorenzo further east).

Aug 8–11

Lisbon & Belém

Three riverside nights with the Hyatt in Belém as your base — monasteries and custard tarts on foot, an aquarium across town, a boat on the Tagus — then north to Fátima and the Douro.

Dates
Aug 8 – 11
Base
Hyatt Regency, Belém
Getting around
15E tram · cable car · Ubers
Shape
2 full days + 2 half
How this leg is built. Belém puts the monasteries, tower, and pastéis within a flat walk of the hotel; the aquarium and cable car sit across town at Parque das Nações. A few things shape the days:
  • Jerónimos & Belém Tower close Mondays — so those go Sunday, and Monday is the aquarium (open daily).
  • The hotel pool is adults-only (spa, 16+, small fee) — no toddler splashing, so downtime means the Belém riverfront and LX Factory, both a short walk away.
  • Book ahead: Jerónimos timed-entry tickets, the Tagus cruise, and the Arrábida kayak.
Day by day

The days

SAT8AUG
Arrival · keep it easy

Drive up, settle into Belém

After the Comporta beach-lunch stop on the A2, cross the 25 de Abril bridge to the hotel by mid-afternoon. The apartment-style suites make an easy landing.

Evening — Stroll the Belém riverfront (Discoveries Monument, gardens), your first pastel de nata at the original Pastéis de Belém a few minutes away, and an early dinner. Adults can christen the rooftop ICON bar at sunset.

SUN9AUG
Belém on foot + the river

Monastery, tower, and a Tagus cruise

Morning — Jerónimos Monastery right at opening (book timed entry; the queue balloons by late morning). It's a working church and a Manueline masterpiece — worth lingering in. Then the Discoveries Monument, the Belém Tower exterior, and the waterfront gardens — all flat and walkable.

Midday — Lunch and a downtime block (hotel or riverfront), plus more pastéis.

Golden hour — A private Tagus river cruise from Doca do Bom Sucesso, right by the hotel: gliding past Belém Tower, the bridge, and Cristo Rei is a hit with the kids, and a private boat fits all 11 at your own pace. Bring light layers — the river is breezy.

MON10AUG
The big kid day

Oceanário & Parque das Nações

Full day — The Oceanário is the city's top kid-pleaser and open daily (the smart Monday move, with Belém's monuments closed). Cluster it with the hands-on Pavilhão do Conhecimento science museum and the cable car over the river. Lunch on-site. ≈25 min from Belém

Adults-only (or split-family) kayak window: a natural morning for a couple of adults to slip away to Arrábida while the rest of the crew does the Oceanário — see Adults only. Otherwise, the evening spa + rooftop is the easy option.

Oceanário tickets
TUE11AUG
Travel north · pilgrimage stop

Fátima, then the Douro

Morning — Leave early up the A1 to Fátima (~1h15; free entry, free parking behind the Basilica of the Holy Trinity). One of the world's great Catholic pilgrimage sites, and a genuinely moving stop: the Chapel of the Apparitions, the Basilica of Our Lady of the Rosary (tombs of the three shepherd children), and the vast Holy Trinity basilica, with candles to light. Arrive earlier for calm — and the 11th sidesteps the 12th–13th pilgrimage crush.

Optional — One add-on that fits the northbound route: Batalha Monastery (~25 min, a jaw-dropping Gothic UNESCO site) or a Coimbra lunch stop. Cap it at one.

Then ~2¾ hr on to the quinta near Pinhão — a ~5–6 hr travel day all told. Phase 3 picks up here.

Adults only · Lisbon

A half-day window or two is realistic here — not a full day. These fit that gap.

Arrábida sea-kayak Adults · or split family

Paddling only — no coasteering or cliff jumps. You glide through crystal water and hidden sea caves in the Arrábida marine reserve, with a swim stop at the secluded Praia do Ribeiro do Cavalo. Tandem kayaks, small group, doable for all levels.

Sesimbra, ~1 hr from Belém · ~3-hr tour · morning slot (less wind) · all gear included

Budget a generous half-day. With ~1 hr each way it runs roughly 8am–mid-afternoon — best slotted Monday morning while the rest of the crew is at the Oceanário.
Arrábida kayak · Viator

Spa + rooftop, at the hotel Zero travel

The lowest-effort window: the adults-only Serenity spa and thermal pool downstairs, then sunset drinks at the ICON rooftop over the Tagus, the bridge, and Cristo Rei. No car, no planning — ideal for the second half-day.

Fado night in Alfama

An evening of Portugal's soulful, melancholic song over dinner in the old quarter — the most Lisbon thing you can do once the kids are down.

Fine dining optional

Optional, if the mood strikes: Feitoria (1 Michelin star, right in Belém — the convenient one), or the two-star Chiado pair Belcanto and Alma. Book well ahead.

Optional whole-crew day trip

Sintra — everyone, at your own pace

With multiple cars, Sintra works as a full-family day and stays flexible: anyone who's had enough of the hills and heat — the littlest, or an adult ready for a break — can peel off early in one car while the rest carry on. The palaces are magical but hilly, stair-heavy, and crowded in August, so the trick is to start early, book timed tickets ahead, cap it at two sites, and keep the pace relaxed rather than a march.

Morning · the palaces

On the road from Belém by ~8am (~40 min) to beat the parking crush. Pena Palace first, at opening — the candy-colored terraces are the showpiece; a carrier beats a stroller on the park paths for the 1½-year-old. Then either Quinta da Regaleira (the Initiation Well and mossy tunnels are a hit with the older kids) or gentler Monserrate gardens if the little ones need easier ground. Lunch in the historic centre — pastries at Piriquita.

Afternoon · the coast

Drop down to Cabo da Roca — the westernmost point of continental Europe, dramatic, quick, and free — then Cascais for a relaxed seaside late afternoon, ice cream, and an early dinner before the drive back. This is the natural bail-out point too: a car can run tired kids or adults back to Belém whenever, while everyone else lingers.

How it fits: run Sintra on Sunday and regroup for the whole-crew Oceanário on Monday. Book Pena and Regaleira timed tickets well ahead — peak August sells out — and pack comfortable shoes, hats, water, and sunscreen for all the cobbles and stairs.

Aug 11–14

The Douro Valley

The slow finish: terraced vineyards, a river and a historic train, a hilltop sanctuary, and a valley-view pool to come home to — then the long, scenic drive back to Lisbon for the flight home.

Dates
Aug 11 – 14
Base
Quinta de Santa Marinha, Castedo
Getting around
Car essential
Shape
2 full days + drive out
How this leg is built. This is the unwind at the end of the trip, so the pace is gentle — and the valley is the hottest stop, so mornings are for outings and afternoons for the pool.
  • The base is remote — few restaurants nearby, so lean on the quinta's on-site chef for dinners and book anything outside well ahead.
  • Check-out is Friday. You've got Friday morning in the valley, then the ~3.5–4 hr drive to Lisbon for a Saturday-morning flight — so Lamego is folded into the drive out.
  • Book ahead: the historical train (Wednesday), the Pinhão boat, and any winery tasting.
Day by day

The days

TUE11AUG
Arrival · unwind

Roll in and exhale

After the drive from Fátima, there's nothing on the agenda but the valley-view pool and an on-site quinta dinner. Let the long travel day dissolve.

WED12AUG
Village morning + the historic train

Favaios, then rails along the Douro

Morning — Favaios (~15 min): watch the four-cornered Moscatel bread come out of a wood-fired oven, wander the family-friendly Bread & Wine Museum, taste the sweet Moscatel, and catch the view from the Santa Bárbara chapel. An easy, hands-on couple of hours. Back for lunch.

Afternoon — The Douro Historical Train from Régua (~30 min): a 3pm reception with Port and live music, departure at 15:32 to Tua, a stop at Pinhão's tiled station, and back into Régua by 18:22. Kids love the ride. €60 adult · €32 kids 4–12

Note for 2026: it runs with a restored 1960s diesel locomotive (not steam) due to line works — same historic carriages, music, and scenery. Book seats ahead.

Historical train · CP
THU13AUG
On the river + farewell dinner

Pinhão, a boat, and a winery

Morning — Into Pinhão (~15 min) for a rabelo boat ride on the Douro and the famous tiled station, then Quinta do Bomfim right there — it has a small museum and a playground, so a tasting works with little ones underfoot. Lunch in Pinhão.

Afternoon — Pool and shade through the heat. (An adults-only premium tasting slots in here — see Adults only.)

Evening — Your Douro farewell: dinner at DOC, chef Rui Paula's riverside room at Folgosa (~25 min) — or keep it easy with an on-site quinta dinner.

Pinhão boat · Magnífico Douro
FRI14AUG
Check out · drive to Lisbon

Lamego on the way south

Morning — Check out and make Lamego (~40 min) your first leg — it sits right on the road south. The Sanctuary of Our Lady of Remedies crowns a monumental staircase of 686 tiled steps: anyone up for it can climb, while others drive to the top and meet them — or ride up and walk down. Add the cathedral, then point the cars toward Lisbon.

On the way — It's ~3.5–4 hr all in, so break it with lunch: Coimbra (the historic university city, most directly on the route) or, for a flatter, kid-pleasing stop, Aveiro with its canals and colorful moliceiro boats.

Arrive Lisbon in the evening; an airport-area hotel makes the Saturday-morning flight home easy. Keep Lamego brisk — it's a big travel day.

Eat & drink. The marquee meal is DOC (Rui Paula, on the river at Folgosa). Around Pinhão, Cozinha da Clara (Quinta de la Rosa) and Veladouro are lovely lunches; Castas e Pratos is the modern pick in Régua; and the quinta's own kitchen is your easy dinner given how remote you are. Flavors worth chasing: Moscatel de Favaios and its four-cornered bread, Douro reds and Port, roast cabrito (kid goat), posta (Mirandesa beef), bola de Lamego (ham bread), and the region's olive oil.

Adults only · Douro

With Friday now a travel day, the one real window is Thursday afternoon.

A premium tasting Adults · or split family

The grown-up counterpoint to the family-friendly Bomfim visit: a proper tasting at a showpiece estate. Quinta do Crasto is the standout for its famous infinity-pool terrace hanging over the valley; Quinta Nova is the elegant alternative.

~15–40 min from base · book ahead · best on Thursday afternoon while the crew's at the pool

Dinner at DOC

If you'd rather the farewell be a quiet grown-up affair, DOC on the river is the place — chef Rui Paula's tasting-driven cooking with the Douro sliding by. Equally good as a whole-family lunch on the terrace if you'd rather share it.

Optional alternative · a whole day

Bom Jesus do Monte (Braga)

Be clear-eyed about this one: Braga is ~2 hours each way from your base, so Bom Jesus is essentially a full day — and with your tightened schedule it wouldn't be an add-on, it would have to replace the train day or the Pinhão day. Since Lamego on your way out already delivers a very similar hilltop-sanctuary experience 90 minutes closer, this is genuinely optional.

If you do go: it's a UNESCO Baroque sanctuary where the world's oldest water-powered funicular (1882) carries you to the top in about three minutes for a couple of euros — so no one has to climb the 573-step Stairway of the Five Senses unless they want to. Entry is free, and you can pair it with Guimarães, 15 minutes away.
